Behaviour

How the face reacts

Device state modulates whatever emotion is currently set, so the face stays expressive while still telling you what the hardware is doing.

StateWhat you seeDriven by
IdleSlow drift and breath, blinking every 2–6 s so it never looks frozen.Free-running clock
ListeningEyes widen 16%, brows lift, mouth closes and the gaze rises slightly.State change
SpeakingMouth opens in step with the voice — fast attack, slow release. Measured on hardware: r = 0.982 against the speech envelope, travelling 3.3–42.7 px across 66 distinct apertures.RMS of PCM at the speaker
ThinkingEyes flick between two upper corners, the way people search memory.Emotion + clock
ConnectingNarrowed eyes sweeping side to side, brows hidden, mouth flat.State change
Fatal errorHard squint, steep brows, deep frown.State change
Verified on the device, not just in code. The board was woken by speaking “Jarvis” aloud, held a real exchange with the server, and the speech envelope and mouth aperture were traced together at 30 fps throughout. Over 3.1 s of speech the mouth took 66 distinct values and correlated with the audio at r = 0.982; the trace shows it snapping 6→37 px in two frames on a syllable onset and easing back over five — the intended fast-attack, slow-release envelope.

Changes

Six files

  • main/display/robot_face_display.cc / .hnew

    Expression table, frame loop, easing, blink scheduler and the LVGL geometry for eyes, brows and mouth. Also carries two bring-up aids behind a compile flag: a framebuffer dump and a direct-to-panel identify blink.

  • main/audio/audio_service.cc / .hmodified

    Samples the RMS of each PCM buffer as it is handed to the codec and publishes it as GetPlaybackLevel(), so the mouth follows real speech instead of a timer.

  • main/boards/lckfb/szpi-esp32s3/lichuang_dev_board.cctwo fixes

    SPI mode 2 → 0, without which this panel displays nothing at all. Plus a guard so a missing FT5x06 touch controller no longer panics at boot. Also builds a RobotFaceDisplay in place of the stock display.

  • main/CMakeLists.txtmodified

    Adds the new source to the build.

The bug that hid the face

A panel that acknowledged everything and displayed nothing

The first build flashed, booted, connected and rendered a perfect face — onto a screen that never changed. Everything reported success:

SignalSaidMeant
lv_snapshot_takeCorrect faceOnly that the widget tree was right — it re-renders, it never reads the panel
LVGL flushflush_start = flush_finish = 1481LVGL handed the pixels off and they were accepted
esp_lcd_panel_draw_bitmapESP_OKOnly that the SPI transaction was clocked out
I²C scan0x18, 0x19, 0x41Codec, PCA9557 (LCD reset) and mic ADC all alive
The board profile sets io_config.spi_mode = 2; this panel only latches in mode 0. Wrong clock polarity means the ST7789 never registers a valid byte, while the ESP32 clocks bits out perfectly happily and every layer above reports success. Because the panel keeps its GRAM across a soft reset, the screen went on showing the previous firmware's Chinese UI — which read as "the flash didn't take". The pin-identical zhengchen/cam profile for this same hardware uses mode 0. One line.

What settled it was writing straight to the panel, underneath LVGL, and blinking the whole screen red and white — the one test whose result could not be faked by a layer reporting success.

Wake word, and where the voice lives

RequestStatus
Wake wordDone. wn9_jarvis_tts — say "Jarvis". Confirmed in the AFE pipeline: [input] → |AEC| → |VAD| → |WakeNet(wn9_jarvis_tts)|
VoiceNot a firmware setting. The device has no TTS engine; it only plays Opus streamed from the server, so accent, pitch and rate belong to the agent in the xiaozhi console.
Measured from your sample (3.63 s, 74% speech): median F0 139 Hz, p10–p90 spread 10.9 semitones (animated, not flat), rate 6.0 syllables/sec — brisk against the 4–5 conversational norm. For British English male, ask for en-GB-RyanNeural or en-GB-ThomasNeural at rate +15–20%, pitch default: Ryan sits close to 139 Hz naturally, so the rate is the real change.
